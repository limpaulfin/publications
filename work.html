What I build, and why

AI you can check.

The problem

AI talks well. It does not always tell the truth.

Ask it to add up a bank's numbers, and it guesses. Ask it about your documents, and it may invent an answer. It sounds sure either way.

What that costs

People stop trusting it, or they trust it too much. Both are expensive.

A student gets an answer and learns nothing. A banker gets a number and cannot check it. A wrong number you believe is worse than no number.

My answer

Three simple rules.

  1. Make it read the right sources. Retrieval (RAG) gives the model real documents to answer from. I built this for tutoring (EduMSRA) and for questions that need several hops of reasoning (AgenticGraph-RAG).
  2. Never let it do the math. A small, exact calculator does the numbers. The model only decides what to ask. This is the idea behind my accounting and audit benchmark, "Computation, Not Recall".
  3. Say how sure you are. Bayesian methods give a range of belief, not a fake yes or no. I use them for bank credit risk, bank stability, and marketing decisions.

Under the hood is applied math: fuzzy decision methods, stochastic stability, causal and uplift analysis. In education, the same thinking shapes studies on prompt quality, digital divides, and knowledge tracing for students.

The takeaway

Good AI shows its sources, does its math with a calculator, and admits what it does not know.

One small step: pick one decision you make every week. Ask three questions. Where did this come from? Who did the math? How sure are we?

Where this can fail

Retrieval is only as good as its sources. If the source is wrong, the answer is wrong, and it will still sound sure. That is a boundary of the first rule.

The calculator adds time, and the model can still ask it the wrong question. That is a failure case of the second rule.

A Bayesian range depends on the starting belief you choose. Pick a bad one, and the range misleads. That is a failure case of the third.

What would prove me wrong: on fresh problems the model has never seen, a model with the calculator does no better than one without. My benchmark is built to run exactly that test.
